privacy policy

Last updated: October 21, 2026

Clearance is a plugin for SCP: Secret Laboratory game servers that checks joining players' accounts. clearance-central is an optional service that lets servers running it share ban reports and anonymous statistics. They are run by different people, so this policy has two parts: what the plugin stores on each game server, and what the central server stores.

tl;dr

  • Each game server keeps its own records about you, like play time, Steam profile details, check results and bans. That server's staff are responsible for them.
  • If you have Do Not Track on, a server forgets your play time, Steam data and check results when you leave, unless you opt in with .trackme.
  • The central server only stores bans: your account id, when the ban was issued, how long it lasts, and a severity and category. It never stores names, IP addresses, chat, play time, Steam data or scores.
  • When you join, the server can ask how many running bans you have on other servers. It doesn't learn which servers they were, and it decides for itself what to do with the answer.
  • Statistics are counts only, with no account ids. Ban reports are deleted after 2 years.
  • To see, fix or delete your data, ask the game server's staff about part 1, or furry<at>furry.coffee about part 2.

part 1: on each game server (the plugin)

who is responsible

The plugin runs on game servers owned by independent operators. Each operator is the data controller for what the plugin stores on their server; contact that server's staff (for example through its Discord or website) with questions about it. The authors of Clearance do not receive this data.

what the plugin stores

When you join a server running Clearance, it may store, on that server:

  • your account id, when you were first and last seen, how often you joined, and how long you played there
  • details from your Steam account, fetched through the Steam Web API: whether the profile is public, account creation date, Steam level, number of games owned, SCP: SL play time, and whether the account has VAC or game bans (and how long ago)
  • a log of each check: the score, the outcome (allowed, flagged, rejected) and how the score was calculated
  • staff actions: bans (including reason, length and who issued them), pardons, the allowlist, sponsorships between players, and strikes against sponsors
  • whether you used .trackme, and when

This is used to keep alternate accounts and banned players out, and to let trusted players vouch for new ones. The legal basis is the server's legitimate interest in protecting its community (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). Scores and Steam data stay on the server. They are never sent to the central server.

do not track

If Do Not Track is on in your game settings, you are still checked when you join, but your play time, Steam data and check log are deleted from the server when you leave (a copy of the Steam data stays in the server's memory for up to 12 hours by default, so it doesn't have to be fetched again if you rejoin). Moderation records (bans, pardons, the allowlist, sponsorships and strikes) are kept, because they are needed for server security. You can opt in to tracking with .trackme in the console, so that your play time counts towards your score, and opt out again with .untrackme, which also deletes what was tracked.

what leaves the server

  • Steam: your Steam id is sent to Valve's Steam Web API to look up the details above
  • Discord (if the server sets it up): messages in the server's staff Discord channel that include your account id when you are rejected or flagged (with your score and how it was calculated), when you are new and couldn't be checked, when you sponsor or are sponsored, and when staff act on your account (bans, pardons, allowlist changes, including any reason staff entered)
  • the central server (if the server takes part): your account id when you join, to look up running bans; your bans of at least a day (by default), and their pardons; and, if the server opts in, daily counts with no account ids. See part 2

how long it is kept

Apart from the Do Not Track deletion above, the plugin keeps its records until the server's staff delete them. Ask the server you played on how long they keep them.

part 2: the central server (clearance-central)

who is responsible

The data controller for the central server is syrenaaa. Contact: furry<at>furry.coffee (replace <at> with @). This service is not affiliated with or endorsed by Northwood Studios.

what we store about players

Only ban reports, sent by the game server that issued the ban. A report contains:

  • your platform account id (Steam, Discord or Northwood id)
  • when the ban was issued and when it expires (or that it is permanent)
  • a severity from 1 to 5 and a category (cheating, griefing, harassment, ban evasion or other)
  • which participating server sent it, and that server's own reference for the ban
  • whether the ban was later pardoned

We do not store names, IP addresses, chat messages, ban reasons, play time, Steam profile data, or the scores the plugin computes. When a server looks up your account id as you join, we answer and do not store the lookup, so we keep no "first seen" or join history.

why, and on what legal basis

Ban reports are used to protect game servers against cheaters, harassment and ban evasion. The legal basis is legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR): the interest of server operators and their players in a safe community. Ban reports are kept to the minimum needed for that purpose and are not used for advertising, profiling or anything else.

who can see it

When you join a participating server, that server can ask us about your account id. It receives only a summary: how many running bans you have, from how many different servers, and the date, expiry, severity and category of the most recent one. It does not learn which servers banned you. Expired and pardoned bans are not counted. Each server decides for itself what, if anything, to do with that summary; we never tell a server to allow or reject anyone.

Only registered servers can make these requests. When a server's access is revoked, its reports stop being shown.

what we store about server operators

For each participating server: its public address and port (as listed on the SCP: SL server list), when it joined, whether its access was revoked, and a one-way hash of its API key. If you host a server from a home connection, that address may identify you; it is used only to verify and manage the server's access and is never shown to other servers.

statistics

Servers that opt in also upload daily counts (for example, how many joins were allowed or flagged, and how many fell into each score range). These contain no account ids and no personal data. Days with fewer than 20 checks are not stored, and counts are only published as sums over days where at least five servers reported, on the stats page.

how long we keep it

  • ban reports: deleted automatically 2 years after the ban was issued, together with any pardon
  • daily statistics: deleted automatically after 2 years
  • server ownership claims: deleted after 1 hour

where it is hosted

The service runs on Val Town, which stores the database and may be located outside your country, including in the United States. Like any web host, Val Town and its network providers process technical request data to deliver and protect the service, such as the requesting server's IP address and the requested address (which, for a lookup, contains your account id). This may appear in their short-term logs; we do not store it in our database.

your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask to see the reports stored about your account, have incorrect ones corrected, have them deleted, or object to their processing. Email furry<at>furry.coffee with your account id. We will ask you to prove you own the account before acting, and we reply within one month. If a report came from a server you think banned you unfairly, you can also contact that server directly; a pardon from them removes the ban from the summary.

You can also complain to a data protection authority in the country where you live.

changes

If this policy changes, the new version will be posted here with a new date.
